    Features

    An eight-channel generative groovebox on Zynthian, played entirely from a Native Instruments Maschine MK2 — pads, encoders, both displays, LEDs. Five euclidean drum channels and three Turing-machine voices, all eight always alive; nothing is created or torn down while playing.

    The generator owns the pattern. You do not draw a beat. You set hits, rotation and randomness, and the driver writes real notes into Zynthian's own sequencer — so patterns persist in snapshots and the touchscreen pattern editor mirrors exactly what the pads show.

    What the instrument does. How you operate it is Playing; how it is built is Internals.

    The generator owns the pattern

    You do not draw a beat. You set hits, rotation, chance and swing, and the driver writes real notes into Zynthian's own sequencer. Because they are real notes in the real sequencer, patterns persist in snapshots and the touchscreen pattern editor mirrors exactly what the pads show.

    The five drum channels are euclidean: hits spread as evenly as the grid allows, then rotate. Four hits in sixteen is four-on-the-floor; five is the clave.

    The three voices are Turing machines. Each owns a shift register, clocked once per pass and mutated with probability RANDOM, and the register's value becomes pitch. Set RANDOM to zero and the loop you are hearing freezes bit for bit, for as long as you leave it there — the display reads LOCK rather than a number, because a number could be a coincidence.

    MIXER and FILTER are spread pages: one control across all eight channels rather than eight controls on one channel. STEP and MIXER carry more than one page; the arrows beside the display step through the ring, and each mode remembers its own page per channel kind.

    A knob with no source draws dead. Where a control does not exist — a drum channel has no CUTOFF — the column greys out rather than showing a number the encoder cannot move.

    A channel's kind is switchable

    SHIFT + GRID switches the selected channel between drum and voice behaviour. The engine is never swapped. A drum kit gets the Turing generator, and on a sampler the register walks the kit's own note list rather than a scale, because there a note selects a sample and scale-quantising would land most steps on empty keys. Euclid on a synth becomes a root pulse. Switching back clears the override; pattern length and division deliberately do not move, because they are pattern time rather than kind.

    Live capture

    Pads are the instrument in every mode but STEP. Holding REC overdubs into the same pattern the generator writes: strikes quantise to the nearest step, and a note's length is how long you held the pad.

    Recording adds, it does not overwrite. The generated line freezes and the played note goes on top; played-in steps light amber. A captured note makes you the owner of that channel's pattern and the generator stops writing it. Two gestures hand it back, both destructive — ERASE with a Group button, or turning any knob that rewrites the pattern.

    Per-channel sound

    Every channel of both kinds has LEVEL, REVERB and DELAY on encoders 6, 7 and 8 — the same three, in the same place, in either mode. Sixteen post-fader inserts carry the reverb and delay sends.

    FILTER spreads CUTOFF and RESO across the row, but only the three voices answer it: the drum channels run a sampler with no filter to reach, so their columns draw dead. A drum filter is not in this version.

    The ALL page holds the global reach: root, scale, BPM, master level, reverb size and type, delay time and feedback.

    The controls follow the plugin

    A voice's CUTOFF, RESO, ENV and DECAY are bound to the synth that is actually loaded, not to the one the snapshot shipped with. Swap a chain's engine from the touchscreen and those four columns rebind to the new plugin's own ports; a role the plugin has no port for draws dead rather than pretending.

    Everything else a plugin publishes fills further CONTROL pages, eight ports per page, in the plugin's own order — so a synth with forty parameters is reachable from the surface without anything here knowing its name. Host plumbing is filtered out, and each port is driven as a percentage of its own range.

    Values that move on their own

    MOD is the SWING button. Hold it and every encoder stops setting a value and starts setting how that value moves. Tap it instead and it latches, so both hands are free. It is not a mode: it adds no page, no ring entry, and nothing to remember. The map of parameters you already know does not change — MOD gives every one of them a second dimension in the same place.

    What answers: LEVEL, REVERB, DELAY, CUTOFF, RESO, ENV and DECAY, plus any port a plugin publishes on a generated page. What refuses: everything that rewrites the pattern — HITS, ROTATE, DENSITY, CHANCE, VELO, GATE, DIVIDE, LENGTH, KIT, PRESET, ROOT and SCALE. Those columns draw dead under MOD, the same way a knob with no source does.

    The refusal is the design, not a gap. A modulator writes several times a second; a knob that rewrites the pattern would tear the line up continuously, and on a channel you had recorded onto it would destroy the take with nothing on the surface saying why. Generation moving on its own is a later feature with its own answer to that question.

    Seeing a value move

    Reading it. A modulated column's name gains a trailing ~, and its bar grows a dashed span across the range the modulator covers with a small tick travelling inside it. The value cell keeps showing the base — the number the knob still steers — rather than chasing the sweep. A fast modulator blurs into a static span, which is honest: the span is the truth and the tick is a courtesy.

    The pads belong to MOD while it is active, so they neither sound nor record until you let go. REC unlights whenever recording is impossible — under MOD, and in STEP mode.

    It costs nothing. No plugin is added, no chain changes, and nothing is created or destroyed while you play.

    The surface talks back

    Both displays carry a tab row: A KICK B SNAR C CLAP D CHAT on the left, E OHAT F BASS G LEAD H PADS on the right, with four encoder columns under each. Group buttons light in channel colours, brightness tracking each channel's level. Peak meters run live.

    A dashed tab means that channel is not sounding — muted at the mixer, or silenced by its own generator. It is the instrument's one mechanism for explaining silence, so read the tab row before assuming a fault.

    What it is not

    No song mode, no pattern chaining, no sampling, no audio recording. Eight channels, always alive. It is an instrument for playing a single evolving pattern, not a workstation for arranging one.
